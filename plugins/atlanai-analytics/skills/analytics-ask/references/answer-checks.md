# Check the explanation as well as the answer

Every numerical statement in an explanation needs a result or arithmetic from returned
values. A correct headline number does not validate its explanation. Keep the scope and
units of each contributing value and distinguish a definition rule from a measured effect.

For an exclusion question, first state the actual rule from the selected definition. To say
what the exclusion changes, inspect every term in the formula for the excluded population.
For margin, check revenue as well as each cost component. Zero costs do not imply zero
margin: excluding an order with revenue and no costs removes its revenue contribution.
For rates, check both numerator and denominator. Do not sum subgroup rates or subtract a
cost twice when it is already included in a broader cost total.

If the question needs a numerical impact, run a separate read-only diagnostic comparison
with and without that exclusion, preserving other filters, grain and window. Label the
counterfactual separately from the managed answer; never change the published definition.
Reconcile the difference to component totals. An unchanged winner or ranking does not mean
the total is unchanged. Sample equality does not prove general semantic equivalence.

Use existing fresh results when they already contain the needed components. If the effect
has not been measured, state the rule and say its numerical impact is unmeasured. Do not
add reassuring phrases such as "changes nothing" without evidence. Preserve NULL and
empty-population behavior instead of inventing zero. Keep the final explanation concise.
